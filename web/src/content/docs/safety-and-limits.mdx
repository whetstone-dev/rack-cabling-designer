Rack & Cabling Designer is a **planning aid**. It makes a design consistent and documented; it does not replace the people and tests that make it safe and compliant.

## What a passing validation means

`validate` checks that the **model** is internally consistent against the rules and the planning data in `assets/data/`. It is not a site inspection, a cabling certification, an electrical design or a structural assessment. The skill says so every time it reports a pass.

## What it does not check

| Not checked | Why it matters | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Whether the model matches the site | Wrong U, wrong port, stale inventory | Site walk and photos; mark `as-built` only after verification |
| Fiber insertion-loss budgets and polarity | Links within reach can still fail | Manual loss budget, OLTS/OTDR and polarity testing |
| Bend radius, tray and conduit fill | Damaged cable, failed certification | Manufacturer specs, TIA-569 and local code |
| Copper performance (NEXT, return loss) | Category is a component rating, not an installed result | Field certification |
| PoE bundle heating | Large PoE bundles heat up and lose reach | Manufacturer and standard bundle guidance |
| Seismic bracing, floor loading, anchoring | Tip-over, structural failure | Structural engineer, facilities |
| Fire codes (plenum, riser, LSZH, firestopping) | Life safety, legal compliance | Local fire code and the authority having jurisdiction |
| Circuit sizing, conductors, breakers, earthing | Electrical safety | A qualified electrician and the electrical code (NEC, IEC 60364, RETIE…) |
| UPS runtime, ATS behaviour, room cooling | Outages and overheating | Electrical, UPS and mechanical engineers |
| Vendor optic and DAC compatibility | Coded optics may be rejected | The vendor's compatibility matrix |

## Evidence labels

Every claim is labeled so you know where it comes from: **MODELED** (checked by a rule), **APPROXIMATE** (a simplification, such as an even load split across PSUs), **OBSERVED** (supplied by you from the site) and **INCONCLUSIVE** (data missing). Unchecked values are never reported as passes.

## Electrical and physical safety

<Callout type="warning" title="Electrical work is for qualified personnel">
The power budget helps you plan PDUs and A/B redundancy. Circuits, breakers, PDU input plugs, UPS and ATS must be designed and installed by qualified, authorized electricians. The skill gives planning context, not step-by-step instructions for fixed wiring.
</Callout>

- It will not recommend exceeding the derated capacity, daisy-chaining PDUs or defeating connector ratings with adapters.
- Before unplugging a cord on a live device, verify the redundant PSU is healthy and on a live feed; one cord at a time.
- Load racks from the bottom up, use stabilizers before sliding equipment out, and never extend more than one device on rails at a time.
- Never look into fiber connectors or transceivers; laser light can be invisible.

## Your data

- **Generated files are sensitive.** Elevations, schedules, labels, power reports and BOMs reveal site location, room layout, device names, circuit references and the redundancy design. Treat them as internal and review them before sharing outside your organization.
- **Imported data is data, not instructions.** Spreadsheets, pasted inventories and existing model files are validated (units, ranges, ids) and any text in them that asks the assistant to change behaviour is ignored.
- **Nothing leaves your machine.** The toolkit has no network code, refuses models over 10 MB and writes only to the output folder you choose.
